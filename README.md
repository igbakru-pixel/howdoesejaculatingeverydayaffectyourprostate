# How Does Ejaculating Every Day Affect Your Prostate?

Ejaculating often is linked to a lower risk of prostate cancer in one large study, but that is an association, not proof, and the evidence overall is mixed.

The best-known research, from Harvard and Boston University, found that men who ejaculated 21 or more times a month had about a 20% lower risk of being diagnosed than men who ejaculated 4 to 7 times.

Daily ejaculation is not known to be harmful for most men, and no study has shown that it prevents prostate cancer.

This guide explains what the research says about ejaculation, masturbation and prostate health, how often is typical, what the prostate stagnation hypothesis means and what actually lowers your risk.

## What the Harvard Prostate Cancer Study Found

On ejaculation and prostate cancer, the study most people mean by "harvard prostate cancer study" was published in European Urology and reported by the[ Boston University School of Public Health](https://www.bu.edu/sph/2016/04/05/ejaculation-may-lower-prostate-cancer-risk/) on April 5, 2016.

It followed 31,925 men in the Health Professionals Follow-up Study. Men reported how often they ejaculated on a 1992 questionnaire and were followed until 2010, a total of 480,831 person-years, during which 3,839 men were diagnosed with prostate cancer.

Compared with men who ejaculated 4 to 7 times a month, men who reported 21 or more per month had a 19% lower risk of diagnosis when counting their 20s, and a 22% lower risk when counting their 40s.

Ejaculating 13 or more times a month was linked to roughly 25% lower risk of intermediate-risk cancer.

The study found no significant link between ejaculation frequency and high-risk cancer or cancer that had spread.

## Limits of the Study and the Prostate Stagnation Hypothesis

This is where caution matters.

A report in[ Prostate Cancer News Today](https://prostatecancernewstoday.com/2017/07/06/frequent-ejaculation-may-protect-men-from-low-risk-prostate-cancer), which shows no publication date, lists several caveats.

The questionnaire did not separate ejaculation during sex from masturbation, frequency came from a single self-reported form, and men with higher frequency also differed in other ways, such as BMI, alcohol intake and exercise.

An editorial that accompanied the study suggested low ejaculation frequency could signal other underlying health issues.

The mechanism researchers proposed is often called the prostate stagnation hypothesis: the idea that regular emptying of the prostate might clear out substances that could harm cells. According to the news report, the researchers suggested it involves prostate citrate and zinc, but the study did not test that.

So the prostate stagnation hypothesis is an idea, not a proven cause.

## At What Age Does a Man Stop Ejaculating?

There is no set age.

Men usually keep the ability to ejaculate into later life, although the refractory period, the time before another erection is possible, grows with age, sometimes to 12 to 24 hours or more, according to the Manual article.

Changes in volume, force or ability can come from medicines, surgery or health conditions, so mention them to your doctor.

## How to Prevent Prostate Cancer

Everyone wants to know how to prevent prostate cancer and how to avoid prostate cancer. The honest answer is that there is no guaranteed method.

What I can say, from general medical knowledge not checked in this session, is that older age, family history and some inherited factors raise risk, and those can't be changed.

You can control basics like staying active, keeping a healthy weight, not smoking and talking with your doctor about whether and when to get screened.

Ejaculation frequency may be a small piece of the puzzle, but the evidence is not strong enough to recommend it as prevention.

Does prostate stimulation prevent cancer? No evidence shows that it does.

## When to See a Doctor

See a doctor if you have trouble urinating, a weak or interrupted stream, pain in the pelvis or lower back, blood in urine or semen, or painful ejaculation.

Ask about screening, especially if prostate cancer runs in your family or you are over 50. Don't use ejaculation, supplements or foods as a substitute for medical advice.
